Assignment 4 · A Possible Future

Meet
Maya.

Maya is 23 years old and just graduated from a Communication and Media programme. She grew up in a world of infinite scroll, AI-generated everything, and a culture that rewarded hustle over health. She is done. Maya represents the CORESET generation: people who are slowly, deliberately, choosing themselves over the system.

She spends her weekends hiking without her phone, drinks matcha instead of energy drinks, and recently spent three days at a digital detox retreat in the Veluwe. She is not anti-technology. She is anti-overstimulation. She is the future consumer, and she is picky.

Coreset 23 yrs Arnhem Media graduate Anti-hustle Slow travel
Her daily life · 2035

Maya lives in a city that has started to redesign itself around rest. Her neighbourhood has a "Screen-Free Sunday" market where no phones are allowed and everything is analogue. She takes the train to work: not because it is faster, but because it is the only hour of her day she genuinely enjoys. She sits in the quiet carriage, looks out the window, and feels like herself again.

She applies only to companies with transparent values. She asked her last interviewer: "What is your policy on after-hours messages?" She got the job because they had a real answer. She will not compromise on peace.

What Maya wants to know

How will the world look for Maya if the CORESET trend keeps growing? What happens to the brands she relies on, like NS, if this movement succeeds? And what if it fails, and convenience wins instead?

What world will she wake up in?

Assignment 4A

Best case scenarios.

Alice Di Prospero & Valerie Kemunto
CSA-C01 · Trend: CORESET
🌿
Best Case 1 Society Rebuilds Around Rest

By 2035, CORESET has moved from a niche lifestyle to a societal norm. Governments, urban planners, and employers have all responded to a decade of burnout data by making structural changes: workweeks are capped at 32 hours across the EU, urban parks have replaced car parks in city centres, and schools teach "digital literacy" not as how to use apps, but as how to choose not to. The concept of being "always available" has become as socially unacceptable as smoking indoors once was.

Maya thrives in this world. She is not a radical; she is simply a person whose needs are now mainstream. She can find quiet carriages on every NS train, she books digital detox retreats at the same price as a normal city trip, and her employer gives her two "offline days" per month as standard. Burnout is no longer something people whisper about; it is something institutions are built to prevent.

The Coreset generation has made the world more honest. Brands that relied on addiction-by-design have had to rebuild. People are sleeping better. Spending is more intentional. Cafes are noisier but phones are quieter.

In today's world

Digital detox retreats exist but are expensive and rare. Quiet carriages on NS exist but are poorly enforced. The 32-hour workweek is a political discussion, not yet a reality. Maya would want all of this but have to fight for it.

🚆
Best Case 2 Brands Compete on Calm

In this version of 2035, the consumer market has reorganised itself around CORESET values. The most profitable brands are not the most stimulating, but the most restorative. NS has repositioned itself as the "mindful commute" brand, redesigning its trains with natural materials, banning advertising inside carriages, and offering a weekly "slow route" that takes a longer scenic path between cities for the same price as a direct connection.

Maya has genuine choice. She can compare brands on a "Coreset Score," a third-party rating similar to the energy label on appliances, but measuring how much a company's product or service supports rather than depletes her wellbeing. She chooses accordingly. Companies that score low lose young customers fast.

Brands like NS, who have invested early in this transition, are thriving. NS is not just a transport company but a wellness partner, a name people associate with the best part of their day. Their ad campaigns no longer show busy professionals arriving somewhere on time. They show people sitting still, looking out the window, breathing.

In today's world

NS trains carry advertising on every surface. The "quiet carriage" has no sound monitoring. There is no wellness rating for brands. Maya would appreciate what NS is, but wish it was more.

Assignment 4B

What if... it goes wrong?

Alice Di Prospero & Valerie Kemunto
CSA-C01 · Trend: CORESET
⚡
Worst Case 1 Wellness Gets Sold Back to Us

Companies see the CORESET trend coming and move fast, but not with genuine change. They rebrand. Every product becomes "mindful," every app adds a "focus mode," every hotel has a "digital detox package" that still has WiFi in the bathroom. The aesthetics of slowness are mass-produced while the underlying logic stays the same: extract attention, sell more, grow quarterly.

Maya is exhausted in a different way now. She cannot tell what is real from what is performance. NS launches a "Rust Carriage" with wooden panels and no WiFi, but the journey is still crowded, the delays still unaddressed, and the design was made by an AI in three hours to capitalise on a trend. The authenticity is gone before it was ever there.

Wellness-washing becomes the new greenwashing. Brands that do it face backlash but recover quickly, because consumers have nowhere else to go. The CORESET movement exists but in a polluted marketplace where everything claims to be calm, and nothing is.

In today's world

We already see this with "self-care" products sold by companies with poor labour practices, and meditation apps that send notifications every 20 minutes to remind you to meditate. This future is not far off at all.

📱
Worst Case 2 Convenience Addiction Wins Instead

CORESET never goes mainstream. The pull of convenience is simply too strong. AI personal assistants, instant delivery, and algorithmically optimised everything make real slowness feel like poverty. Why sit on a slow train looking out the window when an AI has already summarised the view for you? Why be bored when boredom has been engineered out of existence?

Maya still feels the need to slow down, but she is increasingly alone in it. Her employer penalises her for not responding to messages during evenings. Her friends mock her for going to a retreat "like a grandma." Social status is now measured in output, speed, and AI-assisted productivity. Slow travel is a niche for people who cannot afford to be fast.

NS, in this world, is struggling. Young people fly if they can and use fully automated pods if they cannot. The train is seen as a relic. NS never made the pivot to becoming a "mindful travel" brand, and now the moment has passed. Their market share erodes alongside the attention spans of their former passengers.

In today's world

Screen time keeps rising year over year despite widespread awareness of the harm. The average Dutch person checks their phone 150 times a day. This future is the path of least resistance if nothing changes structurally.

Assignment 4D · Final Scenarios

Maya's difficult choice.

Alice Di Prospero & Valerie Kemunto
CSA-C01 · Trend: CORESET
Maya is 23, graduated, and ready to start her career. She has been offered two paths. Both align with her values. But they ask very different things of her. The CORESET trend is strong enough that she has real options, but not so universal that every company plays fair. Before she decides, she has to think carefully about what she believes and how much she is willing to risk for it.
Option A · The Established Organisation

Join NS as a Communications Strategist

NS is making a genuine pivot. They have hired a "Head of Slowness," redesigned two train lines as Coreset experience routes, and publicly committed to removing all advertising from their quiet carriages by 2027. Maya has been headhunted because they want someone who genuinely lives the values they are trying to communicate. She would have stability, a salary, and a real platform. But NS is large. Change inside a 30,000-person organisation is slow. Will she still be able to see the impact of her work?

If Maya lived in today's world, NS would not yet be making this pivot. She would be joining a conventional transport brand with a quiet carriage that is mostly ignored.

Option B · The Start-Up

Co-found a "Slow Travel" Platform with friends

Her two closest friends from university want to build a platform that curates phone-free train journeys across Europe, complete with offline reading packages, nature route detours, and community meetups at the final destination. No screens, no algorithms, no ads. Just people choosing to be present together. Maya would be a co-founder. The risk is real: most start-ups fail. But if it works, she will have built something that embodies the trend rather than just communicating it for someone else. She would see every outcome directly.

In today's world, this start-up would be extremely hard to fund. Investors still expect growth-at-all-costs. A business built on intentional friction is not an easy pitch.

Maya has a lot to sit with, literally. She will decide on a long train journey, phone in her bag, watching the Netherlands pass by outside the window. Both options are real because the CORESET trend has given people like her actual choices, not just aspirations. What she knows for certain is this: she will not work for a brand that performs rest while selling restlessness. Whatever she chooses, it has to be true.
Assignment 5 · Future-Ready Brand

NS

Nederlandse Spoorwegen is the most-used transport brand in the Netherlands, carrying over one million passengers every day. It already lives inside the CORESET trend's natural territory: slow travel, landscape connection, enforced presence. The question is whether NS will lead this moment or miss it.

Proposed Brand Shift
NS → NS Rust

The iconic NS blue represents speed and utility. A new sage-green identity introduces Rust (Dutch: rest, quiet) as a product line. Blue stays for the functional service. Green marks the intentional experience. It is not a rebrand. It is an extension, and a promise.

How CORESET impacts NS across scenarios
  • Best case: NS becomes the defining brand of mindful travel in Europe. Young consumers choose the train not for speed but for peace.
  • Best case: The NS Rust Carriage becomes a premium product with genuine demand, allowing NS to increase fares without resistance.
  • Worst case 1: NS risks wellness-washing accusations if it redesigns aesthetics without addressing delays, overcrowding, and pricing.
  • Worst case 2: If convenience addiction wins, NS loses its youngest customer base to faster, fully automated alternatives and never recovers.
  • Either way: NS needs to act now. The window for an authentic pivot is open. It will not stay open forever.

Assignment 6 · Visual Storytelling

Why this advertisement.

Alice Di Prospero & Valerie Kemunto
CSA-C01 · Trend: CORESET
Why an advertisement

We chose a future NS advertisement as our visual storytelling device because it puts the CORESET trend directly into action. An ad does not just describe a future: it performs it. It shows what it feels like to live in best case scenario 2, where brands compete on calm rather than convenience. We wanted something that Maya herself would stop and look at. Something that would make her feel seen without trying to sell her anything she did not already want.

Why NS

NS was a natural fit because the train is already the most CORESET form of transport available. It is slow by design. It forces you to sit still. It shows you the landscape. It has no steering wheel to distract you. The train is already Coreset. NS just has not said so yet. Our ad is the version of NS that finally does.

Why "NS Rust" and green

We kept the familiar NS identity but added a new layer. Rust means rest or quiet in Dutch, which captures the trend perfectly without being clinical or wellness-y. The shift from NS blue to sage green communicates the transition from speed to nature, from utility to experience. It is not a rebrand that erases the past. It is an extension that opens a new chapter.

What the ad communicates

The headline "Stop arriving. Start travelling." directly challenges the dominant logic of modern transport, which is about minimising journey time. It flips the value proposition completely: the journey itself is the product. This connects to our evidence wall signals about digital detox tourism, the Scandinavian sleep method, silent walking, and the growing desire for experiences that ask nothing of you.

No WiFi, no ads responds to the Screen Free Sunday signal from our field research
Matcha service responds to our weak signal about people choosing matcha for its calming properties
Scenic routing responds to the weak signal about nature walks physically lowering stress hormones
Coreset Certified is a fictional label we invented that mirrors real-world sustainability labels, showing how this trend could become institutionalised
Our reflection

The CORESET trend is not about rejecting the modern world. It is about finding a sustainable way to live inside it. NS, as a brand, is already halfway there: it just needs to commit. If NS does not lead this transition, another brand will. Our ad is a provocation as much as it is a proposal. It asks: what if the most comforting thing NS ever offered was not a timetable, but permission to stop watching one?
